# Atlanta dance and rink move references

Date: 2026-10-31. Purpose: hand-key short loop clips on Nia (Mixamo rig, quad skates).

## How to read this file

- "Verified" means a cited source states the fact.
- "Animator spec" means a keyable spec that we wrote from the verified description. No source gives these exact counts. Check each spec against a video before you ship it.
- BPM values are estimates. No source in this file states a BPM. Measure the tempo from the track that the game licenses.
- Count in beats. "&" is the half beat. A loop is 2 bars (8 beats) unless stated.
- Mixamo bone names: Hips, Spine/Spine1/Spine2, LeftShoulder/RightShoulder, LeftArm/RightArm, LeftForeArm/RightForeArm, LeftUpLeg/RightUpLeg, LeftLeg/RightLeg, LeftFoot/RightFoot, LeftToeBase/RightToeBase, Head.

## 1. ATL Rydin (rink, signature move)

**Origin.** USA Roller Sports names "ATL RYDIN" as the signature move of ATL style, and says that it is similar to the Columbus, Ohio Stride "with a different Style & Swag". Source: https://www.usarollersports.org/culture-roll

Other sources describe ATL style as "swaggy, ryding" with leg crosses, claps, and stomps on the beat, and say skaters often skate backward to hip-hop and Southern rap. Sources: https://chat.skool.com/rollerskate/skate-styles-2?p=c964faa3 and https://www.rollaskateclub.com/rolla-blog/rolling-in-rhythm-black-culture-and-the-heart-of-skate

**Not verified.** No source gives the foot pattern. The spec below is a stride-based interpretation.

**Animator spec (2 bars, rolling forward).**
- Base: knees bent about 30 degrees, Hips low, Spine1 leans forward about 10 degrees, chest up.
- Beat 1: push with the RightFoot to the side and back. The weight goes on the LeftFoot. Hips drop about 4 cm.
- Beat 2: recover the RightFoot under the Hips. Hips rise. Shoulders roll a small amount to the left.
- Beat 3: push with the LeftFoot. Hips drop.
- Beat 4: recover the LeftFoot. Shoulders roll to the right.
- Beats 5 to 8: repeat.
- Arms: loose. Each arm swings opposite to the push leg. Hands relaxed, near hip height.
- Head: level. A small nod down on each push beat.
- Style: the push lands on the beat. The body bob is the "swag". Keep it smooth, not athletic.

**Skates.** Native skate move. The root moves forward. Use it as the default "cruise with style" loop.

**Content.** No flag.

## 2. The Prep (rink)

**Origin.** USA Roller Sports describes The Prep as a "Repetitive Studder-Step Move going forward", with the left leg crossed behind the right, and a clap to keep the move going. Source: https://www.usarollersports.org/culture-roll

**Verified.** Forward, repetitive stutter step. Left leg crosses behind the right. A clap.

**Animator spec (1 bar loop, rolling forward).**
- Beat 1: RightFoot steps short and forward. Knees bend.
- Beat &: LeftFoot crosses behind the RightFoot. LeftToeBase touches behind and to the right of the RightFoot heel.
- Beat 2: hands clap in front of the chest. Hips dip about 3 cm.
- Beat &: LeftFoot uncrosses and steps forward.
- Beats 3 and 4: repeat beats 1 and 2. The lead foot stays the same (the left always crosses behind).
- Torso: upright. Small twist toward the clap.
- Head: looks forward. A small nod on the clap.

**Skates.** Native skate move. The stutter steps are short pushes. The root keeps a slow forward roll.

**Content.** No flag.

## 3. Cross Step (rink)

**Origin.** USA Roller Sports lists the Cross Step as the key move of ATL style. Source: https://www.usarollersports.org/culture-roll

**Not verified.** The source names the move but does not describe it. The spec below uses a standard skate cross step (one foot crosses over the other in front).

**Animator spec (2 bars, rolling).**
- Beat 1: RightFoot lifts and crosses in front of the LeftFoot. It lands on the left side of the LeftFoot.
- Beat 2: LeftFoot lifts out from behind and steps to the left. The feet are now open.
- Beat 3: LeftFoot crosses in front of the RightFoot.
- Beat 4: RightFoot steps out to the right.
- Beats 5 to 8: repeat. Add a stomp (a hard plant of the foot) on beat 8.
- Knees: bend on each landing.
- Arms: out to the sides at about 45 degrees for balance. Hands open.
- Torso: square to the travel direction. Hips turn a small amount toward each crossing foot.

**Skates.** Native skate move. Works forward and backward. The source "skool" post also lists leg crosses, claps, and stomps for ATL style.

**Content.** No flag.

## 4. Bankhead Bounce (dance)

**Origin.** From Bankhead, a neighborhood on the west side of Atlanta. Popularized in 1995 by "Wassup Wassup" (A-Town Players, with L. "Diamond" Atkins and D-Roc). D-Roc does it in the OutKast "Benz or Beamer" video (1995). Michael Jackson did it at the 1995 MTV VMAs. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankhead_Bounce and https://www.ajc.com/life/arts-culture/hip-hop-50/evolution-of-atlantas-hip-hop-dance-crazes/BOICXZM72JD5PF4TQI4D4SWL24/

**Verified.** Shoulders move up and down, arms bent toward the chest (Wikipedia). STEEZY tutorial: forearms up near the face, fists closed and in line with the shoulders. Shoulders bounce at double time. Count 1: arms cross. Count 2: arms open. Source: https://steezy.co/posts/how-to-do-the-bankhead-bounce

**BPM.** Estimate 90 to 100. Not verified.

**Animator spec (1 bar loop).**
- Feet: shoulder width, weight even. A small knee bounce down on each beat.
- Shoulders: up on each "&", down on each beat (two shrugs per beat). Rotate LeftShoulder and RightShoulder about 8 to 10 degrees up.
- Beat 1: forearms cross in front of the face. Fists closed.
- Beat 2: forearms open to each side. Fists stay at shoulder height.
- Beats 3 and 4: repeat.
- Variation (from STEEZY): one forearm points up, the other points to the side. They switch each beat.
- Torso: an optional small rock forward and back.
- Head: a small nod in time with the shrugs.

**Skates.** Upper-body only, so it works in a straight glide. Lock the legs in a slight bend and let the root roll. Also works standing on toe stops.

**Content.** The move is clean and ties to a real neighborhood. The song "Wassup Wassup" lyrics are not checked. Do not license the song without a lyric check.

## 5. Walk It Out (dance)

**Origin.** "Walk It Out" by DJ Unk, 2006, a snap-era hit. The song took inspiration from the "Poole Palace" dance. DJ Montay produced it. Sources: https://www.kaxe.org/news/2025-01-29/unks-walk-it-out-defined-a-fun-hyperlocal-moment-in-atlanta-rap-history and https://www.ajc.com/life/arts-culture/hip-hop-50/evolution-of-atlantas-hip-hop-dance-crazes/BOICXZM72JD5PF4TQI4D4SWL24/

**Checked again (2026-11-01).** The clip uses a stronger twist (about 25 degrees on the toe stops) and a deeper double bounce.

**Verified (STEEZY).** A knee bounce on each beat, then a double-time bounce. Step right: right toes, right knee, and hips point right; the left toes and knee follow. Then twist the same way to the left. On the right step, the right arm shoots out to the side and the left arm stays near the chest. Switch on the left step. Source: https://steezy.co/posts/how-to-walk-it-out

**BPM.** Estimate 75 to 80, felt at double time. Not verified.

**Animator spec (1 bar loop).**
- Double bounce: knees dip on each beat and each "&".
- Beat 1: twist both feet, both knees, and the Hips about 30 degrees to the right. RightArm shoots straight out to the right side at shoulder height. LeftForeArm folds near the chest.
- Beat 2: hold the right twist. Bounce.
- Beat 3: twist to the left. LeftArm shoots out. RightArm circles back to the chest.
- Beat 4: hold the left twist. Bounce.
- Head: faces the same way as the extended arm.

**Skates.** The foot twist needs grip. Do it on toe stops (heels up, the toe stops pivot), or do it in a slow glide where the skates turn toward each side in small heel swivels. On rolling wheels, keep the twist small (about 15 degrees).

**Content.** Low risk. DJ Unk died in January 2025 (NPR). Do a lyric check before you license the song.

## 6. Swag Surf (dance)

**Origin.** "Swag Surfin'" by Fast Life Yungstaz (F.L.Y.), from Stone Mountain, Georgia, 2009. The group says the dance came from "some guys on the southside of Atlanta". Now an anthem at HBCU homecomings, weddings, and pro sports games. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swag_Surfin and https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/fast-life-yungstaz-swag-surfin/

**Verified.** People link arms and sway from side to side in a wave-like sway and dip. A crowd does it together.

**Video check (2026-11-01).** Frames of "Hip-Hop Social Dances | Swag Surfin'" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GsCgfAfk_hg) show the dancers with their arms over the shoulders of the dancers beside them. The arms are near shoulder height, the elbows bend, and the forearms hang down. The knees bend deep, and the group leans to one side, then to the other. The clip uses this form, not raised arms.

**BPM.** Estimate 70 to 80. Not verified.

**Animator spec (2 bars, solo version).**
- Arms: both arms up and out at about 45 degrees, elbows soft, as if over the shoulders of friends. Or one arm up and one arm out.
- Beats 1 and 2: Hips and Spine sway to the left. Weight goes on the LeftFoot. Knees dip on beat 2.
- Beats 3 and 4: sway to the right. Dip on beat 4.
- Beats 5 to 8: repeat.
- Torso: a side bend of about 10 degrees into each sway. The arms tilt with the torso like a surfboard.
- Head: tilts with the sway. Relaxed.

**Skates.** Works in a straight glide. The side sway on skates reads as a slow carve. Good for a group of NPC skaters in a line.

**Content.** No flag. Strong positive meaning (community, HBCU culture).

## 7. Lean wit It, Rock wit It and the snap lean (dance)

**Origin.** "Lean wit It, Rock wit It" by Dem Franchize Boyz (Atlanta), 2005 to 2006. Snap music started in Bankhead in the early 2000s. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lean_wit_It,_Rock_wit_It and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snap_music

**Verified.** AJC: the snap dance sways left to right and snaps the fingers at the peak of each lean. Wikipedia (snap music): a lean-back with a finger snap. Source: https://www.ajc.com/life/arts-culture/hip-hop-50/evolution-of-atlantas-hip-hop-dance-crazes/BOICXZM72JD5PF4TQI4D4SWL24/

**BPM.** Estimate 75 to 80. Not verified.

**Not found (2026-11-01).** No tutorial or video in a search gave counts for this dance. The clip follows the AJC and Wikipedia text only: a lean back to the side, a finger snap at the peak beside the hips, and a rock of the knees.

**Animator spec (1 bar loop).**
- Beat 1: lean the Spine back about 15 degrees and to the left. Weight on the LeftFoot. Knees bend.
- Beat 2: snap the fingers of both hands at the peak. Hands at about hip height, elbows bent. Shoulders drop.
- Beat 3: lean back and to the right.
- Beat 4: snap.
- "Rock": between leans, a small forward rock of the Hips.
- Head: tilts back with the lean. A cool, relaxed face.

**Skates.** Works in a glide. Do not lean back more than about 15 degrees, because a quad skater falls backward past that. Add a small forward knee bend to counter the lean.

**Content.** Low risk for the move. The song lyrics are not checked.

## 8. Nae Nae (dance)

**Origin.** Created in Atlanta by We Are Toonz (2013). The AJC lists it. Famous2Most members also claim a part in it. Sources: https://www.ajc.com/life/arts-culture/hip-hop-50/evolution-of-atlantas-hip-hop-dance-crazes/BOICXZM72JD5PF4TQI4D4SWL24/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nae_Nae

**Not verified in this research.** No source in this file describes the body mechanics. The spec below is the common form: one arm up, a side-to-side sway.

**Animator spec (1 bar loop).**
- RightArm: raised above the head, hand open, palm forward. A small wave side to side.
- LeftArm: relaxed at the side, or the hand on the stomach.
- Beats 1 and 2: Hips and shoulders sway to the left. Knees bend.
- Beats 3 and 4: sway to the right.
- Head: a small bob on each beat.

**Skates.** Upper body plus sway, so it works in a glide.

**Content.** Low risk. Silentó used the Nae Nae in "Watch Me (Whip/Nae Nae)" (2015). Silentó was later charged with the murder of his cousin (Business Insider: https://www.businessinsider.nl/rapper-silento-known-for-viral-dance-song-watch-me-whip-nae-nae-charged-with-murdering-his-cousin/). Do not use that song. Do not name Silentó. Credit We Are Toonz.

## Moves on hold or not recommended

### Whoop Rico (the user's name: "Whoop Rico")

- **Exists.** The Atlanta duo Famous2Most (Hasani Buchanan and PJ, with Jalen Bear and Rari) say they created the "Whoop Rico", the "Nae Nae", and the "Whip". Sources: https://thesource.com/?p=256488 and https://audibletreats.com/?p=29967
- **Not verified.** We found no description of the body mechanics, no song title, and no year. Do not key it until someone finds a video reference.
- **Content.** No flag found.

### Motorcycle

- "It's Goin' Down" by Yung Joc, 2006. Lean left to right, bounce the shoulders, hands crank like motorcycle handles. Source: AJC (above).
- **Skates.** Very good on skates. Handle crank plus a lean reads as "rev and go".
- **Content.** Lyric check needed. Not checked. The move itself is clean. Good candidate for a 9th slot.

### Laffy Taffy

- D4L, 2005, number 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 in January 2006. Secondhand sources describe hip shakes and air punches. Source: https://www.xxlmag.com/d4l-laffy-taffy-no-1-billboard-hot-100/
- **Content flag.** The lyrics are sexual. "D4L" means "Down 4 Life". Do not use the song or the name. A generic hip-shake plus punch is acceptable.

### Rock Yo Hips

- Crime Mob featuring Lil Scrappy, released August 29, 2006. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Yo_Hips
- **Content flag.** The group name contains "Crime". The song is sexual. Do not use the song, the group name, or the move name. A generic hip rock is acceptable.

### Hit Dem Folks

- Bankroll Fresh, 2015. Source: AJC (above).
- **Content flag: exclude.** "Folks" is a common term for the Folk Nation gang alliance. The name and the hand gestures can read as gang signs. Bankroll Fresh died from gunfire in 2016 (from general knowledge; not verified in this research). A city government would not accept it.

### Dab

- Migos, 2015. Source: AJC (above).
- **Content flag.** "Dab" is also slang for a cannabis concentrate. Low to medium risk. Not recommended for official content.

### Crank That (Soulja Boy)

- Soulja Boy, late 2007, Atlanta scene. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snap_music
- **Content flag.** A lyric in the song has a sexual slang meaning. Do not use the song or the "Superman" name.

### Not Atlanta

- **Stanky Legg.** GS Boyz, from Arlington, Texas (2008). Not Atlanta. From general knowledge; not verified in this research.
- **Dougie.** Popularized in Dallas ("My Dougie" by Lil Wil, 2007), named after Doug E. Fresh. Not Atlanta. From general knowledge; not verified in this research.

### The Yank

- Not verified. One blog mentions "Crank Dat Yank" by Camp22 as one of many local Atlanta "crank that" dances. No mechanics found. Source: https://snobhop.substack.com/p/the-snap-effect-when-atlanta-took

### Rink terms that we could not verify

- **Snake walk.** Atlanta Magazine says Usher and Jermaine Dupri do "the snake walk" at Cascade. No mechanics found. Source: https://www.atlantamagazine.com/50bestthingstodo/roll-soul-cascade-skating-rink/
- **The Chicken, "chicken scratch", "Jackknife".** No source ties these names to Atlanta rink skating. Do not use them as ATL moves. (Note: the "Jack Leg" is an Ohio stride move, not an ATL move. Source: https://www.usarollersports.org/culture-roll)
- **Golden Glide and Skate-Town.** We found no move lists from these rinks. Golden Glide (Decatur) news only: https://www.ajc.com/arts-entertainment/2026/09/atlanta-roller-derby-tries-to-save-decaturs-legendary-golden-glide/
